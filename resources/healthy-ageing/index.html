CLINICAL GUIDE

Body Composition in Healthy Ageing

When stable weight doesn’t mean nothing has changed

Read the guide ↗

01

Look beyond stable bodyweight

A stable weight does not establish that body composition is unchanged. Different components can move in opposite directions. For an older person, the clinical question may concern muscle quantity, but it may also concern strength, independence or function.

These are related questions, not interchangeable measurements. Start by defining what needs to be assessed and which information will support it.

02

Distinguish muscle quantity from strength

The EWGSOP2 consensus places muscle strength centrally in sarcopenia assessment, with muscle quantity or quality used to confirm the diagnosis and physical performance informing severity. A muscle mass result alone is not the complete diagnostic process.

This campaign does not claim that mBCA Alpha independently diagnoses sarcopenia. Use recognised assessment pathways, method-appropriate reference information and professional judgement.

Cruz-Jentoft et al. (2019): EWGSOP2 consensus

03

Check suitability and practical access

Do not assume every older patient can use a standing device. Check balance, the ability to maintain the required position and the current contraindications and instructions for use. Have a clear route for people for whom the method is not suitable.

seca describes validation in older populations. That does not remove the need to check how a study population and measurement method relate to an individual patient or a particular service.

seca UK: clinical validation · seca UK: mBCA Alpha

04

Consider repeat measurement thoughtfully

Where clinically relevant, a baseline and comparable follow-up may add context to review. Record preparation and relevant changes in illness, medication or fluid balance. Do not assume that every difference reflects a change in muscle tissue.

Interpret results with strength, function, nutritional information and the patient’s priorities. Avoid imposing a generic numerical target or describing ageing as a uniform trajectory for every person.

05

Build a humane patient conversation

Ask what matters to the individual, such as everyday activities, confidence or independence. Explain why a measurement is being considered and how it relates to the wider assessment. Ask permission and make room for the person’s concerns.

A result should support a clinical conversation, not define the individual or become a substitute for listening to their experience.

06

Evaluate the role in your pathway

A healthy-ageing service could test measurement at an existing review, with eligibility and clinical ownership defined in advance. Observe accessibility, staff workload and whether the information contributes to the assessment.

Use the evidence to decide where it belongs. Neither repeated measurement nor a device installation establishes improved function or reduced care needs.
