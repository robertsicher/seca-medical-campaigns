Clinical Pilot Framework

A practical eight-stage framework for testing clinical usefulness and operational fit

Read the guide ↗
Clinical education · 14 September 2026 · Printable HTML resource

IN THIS GUIDE

01 · Define the clinical problem02 · Identify the patient cohort03 · Map the existing pathway04 · Define where measurement fits05 · Agree clinical and operational outcomes06 · Run the pilot07 · Review evidence08 · Decide whether wider deployment is justified
01

Define the clinical problem

Write down the question that current information does not adequately answer.

Define the current gap in information and why it matters. Write a focused evaluation question, not a claim of expected savings. Identify who will act on the result and how it might affect a clinical conversation or process.

02

Identify the patient cohort

Agree inclusion, exclusion and suitability criteria with the clinical lead.

Describe the intended cohort and how eligibility will be confirmed. Check the device’s current instructions for use, contraindications and physical requirements. Record reasons patients cannot participate so the results do not conceal exclusion or accessibility barriers.

03

Map the existing pathway

Record present touchpoints, responsibilities, information flows and constraints.

Document the present sequence of appointments, handovers and reviews. Measure the current workload and touchpoints using a proportionate baseline period. Include who receives information, who interprets it and where delays or duplication actually occur.

04

Define where measurement fits

Choose the touchpoint, trained operator, review process and data route.

Specify the operator, location, preparation process, cleaning requirements, review responsibilities and data route. Agree training and support before launch. Ask digital and information-governance teams to assess the real implementation, not an assumed integration.

05

Agree clinical and operational outcomes

Set baseline measures, success criteria and stopping or review rules before launch.

Agree definitions for eligible patients, completed measurements, utilisation and planned repeats. Include clinician and patient feedback, total workflow time, usefulness of information and observed effects on touchpoints. Set success criteria in advance and avoid inferring clinical benefit solely from satisfaction or uptake.

06

Run the pilot

Use an agreed protocol, support the team and record exceptions as well as completed measurements.

Confirm the organisation’s governance route and whether the work is service evaluation, audit or research. Run the agreed protocol and record deviations, problems, incomplete measurements and additional work. Do not collect identifiable patient information in a marketing system or this prototype.

07

Review evidence

Evaluate usefulness, experience, workload and limitations against the original question.

Compare findings with the baseline and original question. Describe the cohort, missing information and limitations. Distinguish what was observed from what can be attributed to the intervention. Consider patient experience and unintended consequences alongside activity levels.

08

Decide whether wider deployment is justified

Expand, adapt, continue testing or stop. Make the decision proportionate to the evidence.

Present the evidence to the relevant buying group. Options include limited deployment, a redesigned pathway, further evaluation or stopping. Any broader business case should include equipment, software, training, workforce, support and ongoing delivery costs, rather than assuming NHS savings.
