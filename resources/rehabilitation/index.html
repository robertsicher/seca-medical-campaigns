CLINICAL GUIDE

Body Composition in Rehabilitation

Measure the rebuild: one possible input within a wider recovery assessment

Read the guide ↗
Clinical education · 14 September 2026 · Printable HTML resource

01

Start with the recovery question

A rehabilitation team may want to understand change in body composition alongside progress in symptoms, strength and function. Total bodyweight alone cannot describe muscle distribution or show that recovery is complete.

Define the specific question and whether body composition information would add something useful to the existing assessment. It should not become a proxy for a functional outcome it does not measure.

02

Consider muscle quantity and distribution

seca Analytics 125 includes total and segmental skeletal muscle parameters. These may be considered where relevant, but differences between segments do not independently establish an injury, its cause or recovery status.

Interpret the result within the complete rehabilitation assessment and avoid assuming that more muscle mass necessarily means better function in a particular individual.

seca UK: Analytics 125 · seca UK: medical body composition applications

03

Put suitability before measurement

A standing device is only appropriate where the person can safely follow the required measurement procedure. Pain, mobility restrictions, positioning requirements or other clinical circumstances may make another approach necessary.

Follow the current instructions for use and local clinical governance. The availability of a body composition parameter is not evidence that the device is suitable for every rehabilitation cohort.

seca UK: mBCA Alpha

04

Create comparable observations

If repeat measurements are justified, plan them around meaningful review points. Record timing, preparation and clinical circumstances. Changes in fluid balance, recent exercise or inconsistent preparation may complicate interpretation.

Use comparable methods and units. Review unexpected findings before attributing them to training, tissue growth or recovery.

seca UK: myAnalytics

05

Combine the information with function

Clinical assessment should continue to use relevant measures of symptoms, strength, mobility and functional performance. Body composition can be one piece of information, not the final judgement on readiness to return to activity.

Explain this distinction to patients so that a favourable numerical change is not taken as permission to progress outside their agreed rehabilitation plan.

06

Test the service fit

A pilot could assess whether the result adds useful context at a planned rehabilitation review, whether suitable patients can complete the process and how much staff time the full workflow requires.

Do not promise faster recovery or better rehabilitation outcomes from introducing mBCA Alpha. Those would require separate evidence. A practical evaluation can first establish whether the measurement has an appropriate role.
