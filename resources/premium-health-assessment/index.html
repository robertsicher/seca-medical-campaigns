BUSINESS-CASE GUIDE

Building a Premium Body Composition Health Assessment

A practical business-case guide for clinically led private health services

01

Where body composition could fit

Start with the assessment your service already provides. Which patients attend, what questions do they bring and which clinically relevant information is currently missing? Body composition may be an additional input, but it should not displace appropriate history-taking, examination or tests.

Map the decision it could support and identify who will interpret the result. A premium service needs a clear explanation of relevance, not simply a longer list of measurements.

02

Creating an enhanced assessment proposition

Define who will be offered the enhancement and why. Describe the additional information accurately, the review that accompanies it and the limitations. Make the choice clear before payment, including whether follow-up is included.

Avoid selling certainty. Neither a favourable body composition result nor a premium package rules out disease. A clinically led proposition makes these boundaries understandable.

seca UK: mBCA Alpha · NICE NG246: overweight and obesity management

03

Initial versus repeat measurement

An initial measurement can establish a body composition baseline. A repeat should have a purpose, such as reviewing change alongside an agreed intervention or a relevant clinical review. There is no automatic requirement for a fixed subscription schedule.

Specify when further measurements may be appropriate, who decides, and what comparable preparation means. If follow-ups are included in the package, do not also count them as incremental revenue in the financial model.

04

Patient communication

Write the proposition in plain language: what will happen, which information may be available and who will discuss it. Explain any preparation requirements and provide an accessible alternative to an app-based results journey.

Where myAnalytics is offered, explain the provider-led access process and who patients should contact with questions. Do not imply that app availability proves engagement or improved outcomes.

seca UK: myAnalytics

05

Operational considerations

Time the complete process: eligibility, preparation, measurement, cleaning, assistance, data review and consultation. The published 24-second BIA period is not the length of an enhanced appointment.

  • Identify clinical ownership and operator training.
  • Check space, privacy, access and safe standing requirements.
  • Agree result review, escalation and follow-up responsibilities.
  • Include digital, security and data-governance requirements.
  • Consider maintenance, downtime and continuity arrangements.

seca UK: mBCA Alpha · seca UK: Analytics 125

06

Revenue modelling

Model incremental value rather than the total package price. Begin with assessment volume per location, the proportion of eligible patients offered the enhancement and uptake among those offered. Multiply annual enhanced assessments by the additional value.

Add follow-up revenue only where the follow-up is expected to occur in the year and carries an extra charge. Vary uptake and demand assumptions to understand how sensitive the scenario is. Do not treat a default value as evidence of willingness to pay.

The opportunity calculator provides gross revenue and, if you enter investment, a revenue-based payback period. It excludes operating costs and is not profit, cash flow or a full investment appraisal. Obtain current equipment and software quotes and include staffing, training, service, finance and tax treatment in a proper business case.

07

Deployment considerations

For a multi-site provider, confirm whether patient mix, staff capability and review processes differ by location. A group-wide average may hide a bottleneck or low utilisation at an individual site.

Set minimum clinical and operational requirements, while allowing local pathway differences. Confirm what data can be shared, by whom, and under which governance arrangements. Treat technical integration as a requirement to verify, not an assumed feature of the prototype.

08

Demonstration, pilot and decision

Use the demonstration to examine the device, relevant parameters and real implementation questions. Use a pilot to test eligibility, uptake, usefulness, patient experience and full workflow time.

Agree what would justify continuing, revising or stopping the proposition. A useful business case can conclude that the enhancement is appropriate only for a narrower cohort or that the service is not yet ready to deploy it.
