EVALUATION BEFORE EXPANSION

Build the evidence your organisation needs.

Wider deployment should follow demonstrated clinical relevance and operational fit.

For NHS services and large healthcare groups, a pilot should answer a specific question. Consider patient benefit, workforce requirements, information governance and the cost of delivering the complete service.

Agree whether the proposal is service evaluation, audit or research and follow the organisation’s governance route. This campaign does not imply NHS endorsement, validated NHS savings or procurement approval.

No predetermined result

A pilot can show that an approach is not appropriate, needs redesign or should remain limited to a particular cohort. A credible evaluation makes room for each of those conclusions.

AN EIGHT-STAGE FRAMEWORK

Make the next decision measurable.

  1. 01

    Define the clinical problem

    Write down the question that current information does not adequately answer.

  2. 02

    Identify the patient cohort

    Agree inclusion, exclusion and suitability criteria with the clinical lead.

  3. 03

    Map the existing pathway

    Record present touchpoints, responsibilities, information flows and constraints.

  4. 04

    Define where measurement fits

    Choose the touchpoint, trained operator, review process and data route.

  5. 05

    Agree clinical and operational outcomes

    Set baseline measures, success criteria and stopping or review rules before launch.

  6. 06

    Run the pilot

    Use an agreed protocol, support the team and record exceptions as well as completed measurements.

  7. 07

    Review evidence

    Evaluate usefulness, experience, workload and limitations against the original question.

  8. 08

    Decide whether wider deployment is justified

    Expand, adapt, continue testing or stop. Make the decision proportionate to the evidence.

WHAT TO OBSERVE

Measure the service, not just the number of scans.

Example pilot measures. Select a proportionate set and define collection methods before starting.
MeasureQuestion it could answer
Eligible patients / completed measurementsWho is eligible, who can be measured and who is not represented?
Utilisation / measurement capacityWhat workload is delivered with the actual staffing and process?
Repeat measurement rateHow many eligible patients complete a clinically planned repeat?
Clinician feedback / information usefulnessDid the result add relevant information to the assessment or discussion?
Patient feedbackWas the measurement understandable, acceptable and accessible?
Workflow impact / operational fitWhat happened to preparation, review time, queues and staff responsibilities?
Observed effect on patient touchpointsWere touchpoints added, unchanged or removed, and why?

Patient benefit is a question to evaluate with an appropriate design. Feedback, uptake and workflow measures alone do not demonstrate improved clinical outcomes.

A SHARED ORGANISATIONAL DECISION

Bring the right people into the pilot.

One organisation.
Different responsibilities.

Develop a shared evaluation brief.

Service manager

Workflow and capacity

Practitioner / dietitian

Transformation team

Service improvement

Digital / IT

Data, security and integration

Procurement

Purchasing and compliance

Finance

Business case and total cost

A clinical sponsor alone cannot resolve every implementation question. Include the colleagues who will use, support, govern and fund the service.
